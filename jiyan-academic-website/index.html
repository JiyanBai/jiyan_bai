About

I am a Ph.D. candidate in Economics at the University at Albany, SUNY. My work examines how workers make employment decisions and how their choices aggregate into labor market outcomes.

My job market paper develops an on-the-job search model with social comparison preferences to study wage acceptance, job-to-job mobility, unemployment, wage dispersion, and welfare.

Beyond academic research, I have worked with large-scale administrative healthcare data, developing reproducible analytical workflows and methods for measuring and forecasting claims completeness.

JOB MARKET PAPER

Rank Matters: On-the-Job Search Model with Social Comparison Behavior

Job Market Paper · Working Paper

I examine how workers' concerns about their relative wage positions affect job search, wage acceptance, and equilibrium labor market outcomes.

Read abstract ＋
This paper studies the role of social comparison in an on-the-job search framework. Using NLSY97 data, I estimate the relationship between relative wage rank and job satisfaction. I then introduce rank-dependent utility and endogenous search effort into a quantitative model of labor market mobility. The model investigates how rank concerns influence reservation wages, job-to-job transitions, unemployment, the wage distribution, and welfare.
