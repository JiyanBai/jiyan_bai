Research

Job Market Paper

Rank Matters: On-the-Job Search Model with Social Comparison Behavior

Working paper

Abstract

This paper investigates how workers’ wage rank within a reference group influences job satisfaction, search behavior, and aggregate labor-market outcomes. Using a longitudinal panel constructed from the NLSY97, I estimate the relationship between relative wage position and job satisfaction. I then develop and calibrate an on-the-job search model with rank-dependent utility. The model suggests that stronger rank concerns raise reservation wages and unemployment, reduce job mobility, compress accepted-wage dispersion, and lower social welfare.
